# Six-point accessibility statement self-check

Tick what your **published** statement already does. Every unticked box is
something a user, auditor or market surveillance authority can point at.

- [ ] **1. A contact route a stranger can reach today.**
  A named mailbox, phone number or accessible form. Not a sales form that
  requires an account.
- [ ] **2. A review date in the future.**
  The date must be in the future, not the date the page was published. If it
  has passed, the statement is stale even if the site did not change.
- [ ] **3. The scope stated explicitly.**
  Domains, subdomains and apps covered. "Our website" is ambiguous when
  checkout, help centre and booking run on different hosts.
- [ ] **4. A conformance target named.**
  The standard and version, usually WCAG 2.2 level AA or EN 301 549.
  "Accessible" without a benchmark cannot be audited.
- [ ] **5. Known limitations listed and still accurate.**
  Specific, dated barriers. "PDFs published before 2024 are not tagged" is a
  limitation; "we are working on accessibility" is not.
- [ ] **6. The enforcement route named.**
  The authority or escalation path users can use if your reply does not resolve
  the issue. Without it, the feedback route has no consequence.

## If you score below 6/6

Start with the gaps a user can see: a dead contact address, a review date in the
past, or a limitation list that no longer matches the site. Those are also the
first things an authority will test.

A structurally complete statement can still be inaccurate. This checklist is
not a WCAG audit and does not test screen-reader, keyboard, zoom or contrast
behaviour.
